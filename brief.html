Rubbish.com.au: marketplace prototype

Rubbish.com.au is an Airtasker-style marketplace for rubbish removal. Customers post a pick-up or request a skip or wheelie bin. Local collectors quote on the job, and the customer picks a quote. The prototype is a clickable front end for showing the concept and the main user flows. It is not a working product: there is no backend, no data storage and no payments.

This brief covers what has been built, how it is put together, and where it falls short. Sections 5 to 9 set out the full scope for version 1, including payments, security and integrations. Section 10 lists what we need in your quote.

2. What the prototype shows

Customer side

  • Post a rubbish pick-up
    • The customer chooses We load it (a collector does the lifting) or You load it (DIY, truck only).
    • They add items with steppers, such as a couch, fridge, mattress, boxes or green waste.
    • Each item visibly loads into a cutaway box truck, with a running "about X% of a full truckload" estimate.
    • They then add an address, access details, a timeframe and photos, and post the job.
  • Request a skip bin quote: choose a size, delivery and pick-up dates, and the placement (driveway or road).
  • Request a wheelie bin quote: supplied and collected, as a one-off or ongoing service.
  • How it works: the steps, the factors that change the price, a list of items we can't take (asbestos, hazardous waste and similar) and an FAQ.

Collector side

  • Jobs board: filter by city, job type and size. Each job has a detail page and a quote form with price, earliest date and a message.
  • Bin requests: collectors can quote on open skip and wheelie bin requests.
  • Collector profiles: rating, completed jobs, reviews, vehicle, service area and verification badges.
  • Become a collector: a 7-step sign-up covering business details, ABN, vehicle, service area, licence and insurance. The pitch is: free to register, no joining fees, quote on jobs in your area, and get paid after every completed job.

Shared

  • Homepage carousels: "Being quoted now" (filterable by city) and "Recently completed".
  • Mobile menu and a responsive layout down to phone width.
  • The v2 flows can be embedded (?embed=1), which is how v1 opens them.

3. How it's built

ItemDetail
index.html (v1)A single static HTML file with inline CSS and JS and base64 logos. It uses hash views (#jobs, #how, #join, #skips). Its hero buttons open the v2 flows in a full-screen iframe overlay. A FLOW_BUILD constant busts the cache.
v2.html (v2)A single static HTML file with a hash router: #/, #/jobs, #/job/N, #/bins, #/how, #/collector/N, #/post, #/join, #/flow/<mode>. In embed mode it talks to its parent with postMessage (close and nav events).
v2.src.html + v2-views.*The v2 source fragments. A small script merges them and inlines the logos to produce v2.html.
Truck loaderThe truck photo (t-boxtruck.webp) sits under a frame overlay (t-boxframe.webp). There are 12 transparent item cut-outs (t-*.webp). Items are placed with simple skyline packing inside a clipped cargo rectangle, and their sizes are scaled from rough m³ volumes.
DataAll jobs, collectors, reviews, completed jobs and bin requests are hard-coded JavaScript arrays.
HostingGitHub Pages with no build pipeline. The files were uploaded by hand.
LibrariesNone, apart from Google Fonts (v1 uses Inter and Inter Tight; v2 uses Archivo). The code is plain HTML, CSS and JS.

Treat this code as a visual and UX reference, not a starting codebase. It was built fast to test the concept. We expect the real build to be a proper app with its own architecture.

Built, but placeholder or unverified

  • All content is sample data. Jobs, collectors, names, ratings, reviews, prices, completed jobs and the homepage stats (97% and $1,500) are illustrative only.
  • The volume estimate is a rough guess.
    • Item volumes are approximate.
    • A "full truckload" is assumed to be 20 m³ and a standard skip 6 m³.
    • The wheelie bin "good for" labels are placeholders.
    • These figures need sign-off from operations before they're shown to customers as estimates.
  • The truck graphic is an approximation. It's a 2D side-on view, so it shows roughly how full the truck is, not a real 3D packing calculation. If too many items are added, the extras are clipped rather than handled properly.
  • No pricing logic. The prototype never calculates a price. Collectors set prices through quotes. Any guide pricing would need to be designed from scratch.
  • Disposal and depots are generic. There's no depot directory, gate-fee data or check that waste goes somewhere legitimate.

Technical and design limitations

  • Two separate codebases. v1 and v2 use different fonts and styling and duplicate logic. v1 shows v2 inside an iframe, so a job posted in the overlay never appears in v1's lists.
  • Images are AI-generated. The hero, the truck and the item cut-outs need replacing with real photography or commissioned illustration before launch. The logo on the truck door is part of the photo, so it doesn't change with the colour switcher.
  • The logo isn't a vector. It was cropped from a JPEG logo pack, so we'll supply proper SVG files.
  • Brand colour isn't final. Gold (#FBC303) is the default, and the switcher exists only to help us decide.
  • Not production-quality code.
    • Each site is one large inline file.
    • There are no components and no automated tests.
    • There's no linting and no CI or deploy pipeline.
    • Logos are embedded as base64, so the file sizes are heavy (about 330 KB each).
  • Limited testing.
    • It has only been checked in desktop Chrome and headless Chromium at phone and desktop widths.
    • It hasn't been tested on real iOS or Android devices, in Safari or Firefox, with screen readers, or against WCAG.
  • No SEO or analytics. There's no metadata strategy, no tracking and no conversion events.
  • Security and privacy haven't been considered. There's nothing to secure yet, but the real build will handle addresses, phone numbers, ABNs, licences and payments.

5.4 Admin (back office)

  • Dashboard: jobs posted, quoted, booked and completed; revenue; fees earned; conversion rate from post to booking.
  • Collector approvals: review documents and approve, reject or suspend collectors. The full audit history is kept.
  • Jobs: view any job end to end and edit, cancel or refund it. All admin actions are logged.

6. Payments

Our assumption is Stripe Connect: Express accounts for collectors, with separate charges and transfers. Collectors' ID and bank checks are handled by Stripe, and card data never touches our servers. If you'd recommend a different provider, tell us why.

6.1 How the money moves

  1. Accept: the customer accepts a quote and is charged the full amount, plus any customer fee, straight away. Funds sit in the platform's Stripe balance.
    We charge upfront rather than placing a hold on the card because card holds expire after about 7 days, and many jobs will be booked further ahead than that.
  2. Price change: an approved increase is charged to the saved card; a decrease is refunded.
  3. Complete: the collector marks the job done and uploads the required photos and proof of disposal.
  4. Release: when the customer confirms, or the job auto-confirms, the collector's share is transferred to their Stripe account and paid out on Stripe's standard schedule.
  5. Dispute: if the customer raises a problem before the job is confirmed, the transfer is frozen until admin resolves it.

6.2 Fees

  • Proposed The platform takes a percentage commission from the collector's quote and/or adds a service fee to the customer. The rates and the split aren't decided, so build it so both can be set in admin, including a fee of zero.
  • Card processing costs are absorbed in our fee, not shown as a separate surcharge.
  • Collectors always see what they'll actually receive before they submit a quote.

6.3 Proof of disposal

Proposed Payment is not released until the collector uploads a tip receipt or weighbridge ticket, or confirms the items were taken for resale or donation (with a photo). This protects us against collectors dumping rubbish illegally, and lets us tell customers where their rubbish went. Admin can waive the requirement for a particular job. Please also quote optional AI or OCR checking that a receipt is real and that its date matches the job.

6.5 Invoices, GST and tax reporting

  • Tax invoices: issued automatically.
    • The collector's invoice to the customer for the job, which must handle collectors who are and aren't registered for GST.
    • Our invoice for platform fees.
  • Collector statements: each one shows the gross amount, our fees and the net payout.
  • ATO reporting: we expect to fall under the ATO's sharing economy reporting regime, which means reporting collector payouts. The data model must capture what's needed for this. Needs confirmation with our accountant.
  • Financial-services licensing: we need confirmation that holding funds before release is covered by the Stripe Connect structure and doesn't need a licence. Needs confirmation with our legal and accounting advisers.
  • Accounting: integration with Xero is preferred. Please quote it as an option.

7. Security, privacy and compliance

7.1 Accounts and access

  • Customers: log in with a one-time code by SMS or email. Passwordless is preferred.
  • Collectors: one-time code or password, with two-factor authentication available.
  • Admin: two-factor authentication is mandatory, and there's a session timeout.
  • Roles: customer, collector, admin and support. Each role sees only what it needs; for example, support can't change fees or payouts.
  • Brute-force protection: rate limiting on logins and codes, and on the post and quote endpoints.

7.2 Protecting customer data

  • Collectors see the suburb only before booking. The street address and phone number unlock only for the booked collector, and access is removed 7 days after completion.
  • Job photos are checked for people's faces and house numbers. Proposed This is optional and can be quoted separately.
  • All data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and hosted in Australia.
  • Card data goes only to Stripe, using hosted payment fields. No card numbers are stored or seen by us.
  • Licences, insurance documents and ID are stored in private storage with expiring links, and only admin can view them.
  • The design must meet the Australian Privacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles: a privacy policy, data access and deletion requests, and data retention rules.
  • A data-breach response plan is needed, so we can meet the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.

7.4 Fraud and platform abuse

  • Fake or duplicate accounts are detected by phone, device and payment fingerprint.
  • Collectors with too many cancellations, no-shows or low ratings are flagged automatically.
  • Stripe Radar handles card fraud, and our chargeback process uses the job photos and messages as evidence.
  • Customers can report a collector, and collectors can report a customer.

7.5 Legal pages (we'll supply the wording, you build the pages)

  • Customer terms, collector terms and the marketplace rules. Collectors are independent businesses.
  • Privacy policy and cookie notice.
  • Prohibited items: asbestos, hazardous waste, chemicals, gas bottles and similar. Collectors must be able to refuse these on site.
  • Acceptance of the terms is recorded with a timestamp and version number.

9. Non-functional requirements

  • Hosting: Australian region, with automated daily backups and a tested restore.
  • Environments: separate staging and production, and we have access to staging for testing.
  • Performance: customer pages load fast on mobile 4G, and the job-posting flow works on older phones.
  • Accessibility: the customer website meets WCAG 2.1 AA.
  • SEO: server-rendered marketing pages, with city and service landing pages we can add and edit.
  • Reliability: error monitoring and uptime alerts.
  • Testing: automated tests on the payment and payout logic as a minimum.
  • Ownership: we own the code, designs and data. The code goes in our repository, with documentation and a full handover.
